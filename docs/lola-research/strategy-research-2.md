# Strategy research round 2: additional return sources under fixed constraints
_Written 2026-10-16. Research only; no code changes. Constraints: long-only spot, no leverage, 2% daily / 4% weekly loss limits, 10% MDD, sleeves 60/30/10, venues IBKR/Kraken/Coinbase._

**Verification:** these returned HTTP 200 on 2026-10-16: NBER w22208, w24877 and w25882 (titles checked), AQR trend page, SEDI, Bank of Canada. Wiley/OUP/Elsevier/SSRN/CIRO/S&P/CRA pages blocked automated fetches (403 or timeout); the DOIs are standard citations, so open them in a browser.

**Overarching caveat (McLean & Pontiff 2016, JF, doi:10.1111/jofi.12365):** after publication, anomaly returns fall by about 58% on average (≈26% from statistical bias, the rest from arbitrage). Haircut every published long-short figure by at least 50%. The **long-only constraint loses the short leg** as well, and that is where much anomaly alpha lives (Stambaugh-Yu-Yuan 2012). So expect the realistic net contribution to be small-cap tilts and timing effects of roughly 0.5–3%/yr. The main value is diversification, not large standalone returns.

Ranking = expected net-of-cost contribution × diversification vs the existing momentum/value/quality/trend sleeves.

## Ranked list

**1. Volatility-managed exposure (Moreira-Muir) across all sleeves.** *Mechanism:* scale exposure by inverse realized variance. Without leverage you can only scale *down* from 100%, which still cuts drawdowns, and drawdowns are the binding 10% MDD constraint. *Evidence:* A, with a debate: Sharpe gains hold for many factors but out-of-sample real-time gains are weaker (Cederburg, O'Doherty, Wang & Yan 2020, JFE). Capacity: unlimited. Costs: low with smoothing or bands. Fit: excellent, since it directly protects the risk limits. Effort S (it extends the existing GARCH/regime sizing). https://www.nber.org/papers/w22208 ; doi:10.1111/jofi.12513

**2. Cash/T-bill yield management.** *Mechanism:* the large idle cash balance from the long-only / risk-limited design should earn the overnight rate. Options: IBKR interest on CAD/USD balances above the threshold; CASH.TO/PSA.TO-style high-interest savings ETFs, or T-bill ETFs (ZMMK, CBIL); USD equivalents. *Evidence:* deterministic, not an anomaly. At ~2.25–3% BoC policy-rate levels this is often the single largest "return" in a mostly-flat book. Costs: ETF spreads ~1bp. Fit: perfect. Risk engine: treat these as cash-equivalents with a liquidity flag. Effort S. https://www.bankofcanada.ca/rates/interest-rates/

**3. FX conversion cost minimization (Norbert's gambit, DLR/DLR.U; IBKR IDEALPRO).** *Mechanism:* retail CAD↔USD conversion costs 1.5–2.5%; IBKR spot FX costs ~0.2bp plus a $2 minimum, and Norbert's gambit with DLR costs only commissions plus a 1–3 day settlement wait. This is saved cost, not alpha, but it is guaranteed. Fit: excellent. Effort S. IBKR makes direct FX the simplest option. Keep DLR for registered accounts.

**4. Post-earnings announcement drift (PEAD) + analyst revision/SUE momentum.** *Mechanism:* investors under-react to earnings surprises. *Evidence:* A historically (Bernard-Thomas 1989). It has **decayed sharply in US large caps** since the mid-2000s (Martineau 2022, "Rest in Peace PEAD", Critical Finance Review) but persists in small caps and in non-US markets with less analyst coverage; Canadian small/mid caps are plausibly favourable. Revenue surprises and analyst revisions add independent information (Jegadeesh-Livnat 2006). Capacity: moderate. Costs: medium, with an event-driven ~60-day hold. Fit: good (the long leg holds up). Needs PIT earnings estimates (I/B/E/S-class data is costly; free consensus data is unreliable). Effort M. Low-moderate correlation with price momentum.

**5. Insider buying (SEDI).** *Mechanism:* open-market purchases by officers and directors, especially clustered or opportunistic buys (Cohen-Malloy-Pomorski 2012, JF), predict returns. Insider *selling* is noise for long-only books anyway. *Evidence:* A/B, with Canadian studies supportive. The effect is stronger in small caps. Data: SEDI is public (filing deadline 5 days). Scrape gently and within its terms, or buy a feed (e.g. INK Research / CanadianInsider). Capacity: small. Fit: good, long-side only. Effort M. https://www.sedi.ca/

**6. Turn-of-month and pre-holiday seasonality.** *Mechanism:* liquidity/flow timing; returns concentrate in days −1 to +3 around month-end (Lakonishok-Smidt 1988; McConnell-Xu 2008 found it in 30+ countries, including Canada). *Evidence:* B, stable but small. As an overlay it means *timing deployment of new cash or rebalances* rather than a standalone strategy. Costs: high if traded actively, about zero when used as rebalancing-date selection. Effort S.

**7. Overnight vs intraday return asymmetry.** *Mechanism:* most of the equity premium historically accrued overnight (Lou-Polk-Skouras 2019, JFE). For long-only investors the actionable form is execution timing: trade at the open and close auctions, and avoid fighting intraday reversal. As a standalone close-to-open strategy, costs (2× spread daily) usually wipe out the edge. *Evidence:* A for the pattern, C for net profitability. Effort S (execution policy only).

**8. Index reconstitution (S&P/TSX quarterly and annual reviews).** *Mechanism:* index funds' forced demand pushes up additions around the effective date, followed by reversal. *Evidence:* the US S&P 500 effect has **nearly disappeared** (Greenwood & Sammon 2022, "The Disappearing Index Effect"). TSX-specific studies show smaller but present effects for additions to the Composite and SmallCap. Long-only: buy predicted additions before the announcement, sell into the effective date. Capacity: small. Risk: prediction errors; the opportunity is crowded. Effort M. Low correlation. Evidence C/B.

**9. Crypto: on-chain/flow and cross-sectional factors (spot, 10% sleeve).** *Mechanism:* crypto momentum (time-series and cross-sectional), network-adoption and attention factors (Liu & Tsyvinski 2021 RFS; Liu, Tsyvinski & Wu 2022 JF "Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency"). Exchange netflows and stablecoin mint/burn as flow signals: C. Funding and basis are already used as signals. Costs: Kraken/Coinbase taker fees of 0.25–0.6% at retail tiers, so prefer maker orders and low turnover. Fit: OK inside the 10% sleeve, but the MDD budget is the binding constraint; crypto alone can hit −50%, so vol-target it. Effort M. https://www.nber.org/papers/w24877 ; https://www.nber.org/papers/w25882

**10. Short-interest signals (CIRO bi-monthly short positions).** *Mechanism:* high short interest predicts low returns (Asquith-Pathak-Ritter 2005; Boehmer-Huszar-Jordan 2010). Long-only use: an **exclusion screen** (avoid the most-shorted names) and a positive tilt toward lightly shorted names with high days-to-cover declines. *Evidence:* A in the US, B in Canada. Data: CIRO publishes twice monthly with a lag. Capacity: fine. Effort S–M. Mainly reduces drawdowns.

**11. Sector rotation via industry momentum (Moskowitz-Grinblatt 1999) using sector ETFs/iShares XIU sectors.** It diversifies the stock-level momentum sleeve only modestly (correlation ~0.5–0.7). Evidence A/B; decayed. Effort S. Low cost via ETFs.

**12. Long-only pairs/stat-arb variants.** *Mechanism:* without shorting, implement it as "relative-value switching": hold the cheaper leg of a cointegrated pair (e.g. bank peers, CA/US interlisted pairs) and switch on spread extremes. Gatev-Goetzmann-Rouwenhorst 2006 returns **declined substantially after 2002**, and the long-only variant keeps only part of the edge. Evidence B/C. Turnover is high. Effort M.

**13. Interlisted CA/US stocks.** *Mechanism:* the same stock in TSX/CAD and NYSE/USD. True arbitrage needs shorting and is competed away within milliseconds by HFT, so it is infeasible. Usable forms: (a) **venue selection**, executing in whichever line has the tighter effective spread after FX cost; (b) lead-lag, where US price discovery often leads, so US moves can be used as a feature for TSX trading (B). Effort S–M. Mostly reduces costs.

**14. Trend/risk-parity overlay (multi-asset ETFs).** *Mechanism:* time-series trend across equity, bond, gold and commodity ETFs. A century of evidence (AQR, Hurst-Ooi-Pedersen) shows crisis alpha. Long-only, unlevered risk parity is bond-heavy and low-return without leverage, so use **trend as a de-risking switch** (cash vs risk asset) rather than a levered portfolio. Evidence A. Effort S. Partly overlaps with item 1. https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing

**15. Tax-loss harvesting (taxable accounts only).** *Mechanism:* realize losses to offset capital gains (at the 50% inclusion rate). Canadian **superficial loss rule**: no repurchase of the *identical property* within 30 days before or after the sale, including by affiliated persons, your spouse and your RRSP/TFSA. Swap into a non-identical ETF tracking a different index (e.g. XIC↔VCN is debated; prefer a different index provider/benchmark). Value is a tax deferral of ~0.5–1%/yr in the early years, decaying over time. It has no value in TFSA/RRSP. Also: very active trading risks CRA treating gains as **business income** (fully taxable), which is material for an autonomous bot. Effort M (ACB tracking already exists). Get advice from a Canadian tax professional.

**16. Dividend capture: avoid as a strategy.** Prices drop by roughly the dividend on the ex-date, and costs plus the eligible-dividend tax treatment rarely net positive (some ex-date anomaly studies exist; evidence C). For non-registered accounts, Canadian eligible dividends are tax-advantaged versus US dividends; US dividends carry 15% withholding (recoverable in an RRSP under the treaty, but not in a TFSA). Use this only as a **tax-location rule**, not a trading signal.

## Not recommended
- Leveraged ETFs: these are leverage, so they violate the no-leverage constraint.
- Options and covered calls: outside spot-only.
- Shorting-dependent anomalies.
- Crypto staking and lending yield: counterparty and regulatory risk (CSA restrictions on Canadian platforms). If it is ever considered, treat it as a separate risk bucket.

## Practical ordering
Cash yield + FX cost (immediate, near-certain) → vol-managed scaling and trend de-risking switch (protects MDD) → turn-of-month / auction execution timing → SEDI insider + CIRO short-interest screens → PEAD/revisions (needs data budget) → TSX reconstitution, crypto factors → long-only pairs. Run each as a challenger with a logged trial count and deflated Sharpe, and expect most to deliver less than the published figures.
